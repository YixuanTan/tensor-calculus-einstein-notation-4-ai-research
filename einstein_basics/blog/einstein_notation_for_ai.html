A 10-minute visual guide to Einstein notation for AI & LLMs — three rules, one Kronecker delta, zero transposes.

0No Physics Required

Myth "Einstein notation means learning covariant/contravariant indices, curved manifolds, and the metric tensor \(g_{\mu\nu}\)."
Reality Deep learning (Attention, MLP, MoE, MLA, RoPE) lives in flat Euclidean space with an orthonormal basis. The metric is just the identity \(\delta_{ij}\), so upper and lower indices are identical — subscripts lose zero information.

General relativity needs upper/lower indices because it works in curved, non-orthogonal coordinates. We don't — so everything below uses plain subscripts and ordinary scalar algebra.

1Three Minimal Rules

Rule 1 · View every tensor as a single indexed scalar

Matrix calculus treats matrices as opaque 2D blocks — and breaks the moment you reach 3D or 4D tensors. Einstein notation focuses on one real number at specific coordinates:

  • Entry \(k\) of a vector \(u \;\to\; u_k \in \mathbb{R}\)
  • Row \(i\), column \(k\) of a matrix \(A \;\to\; A_{ik} \in \mathbb{R}\)
  • Element \((b, h, i, k)\) of a 4D query tensor \(Q \;\to\; Q_{bhik} \in \mathbb{R}\)

Because every symbol is an ordinary real number, commutativity and associativity from grade-school algebra come back to life.

Rule 2 · Free indices vs. dummy indices

This is the single most important idea in the whole notation. Every index in an equation plays exactly one of two roles:

Anatomy of an index equation · matrix multiply
\[ C_{\free{ij}} = A_{\free{i}\dum{k}}\, B_{\dum{k}\free{j}} \]
Free indexi, j
  • Appears on the left-hand side
  • Kept — it defines the output shape
  • Never summed
Dummy indexk
  • Absent from the LHS, repeated on the RHS
  • Summed over its full range (contracted)
  • Disappears from the output
Same equation with the hidden sum written out: \( C_{\free{ij}} = \sum_{\dum{k}=1}^{D} A_{\free{i}\dum{k}} B_{\dum{k}\free{j}} \)

To tell which role an index plays, ask exactly one question:

Does the index appear on the left-hand side?
YES →NO →
Free — keep it, never sum it.
Dummy — it repeats on the RHS, so sum over it.

Rule 3 · Factors commute, so transposes vanish — and 4D comes for free

Matrix notation \[ S = Q K^\top \] 
S = Q.transpose(1, 2) @ \
    K.transpose(1, 2).transpose(-2, -1)
# which axis was which again?
Transpose \(K\) just to make inner dimensions touch; 4D needs prose like "per batch, per head".
Einstein notation \[ S_{\free{ij}} = Q_{\free{i}\dum{k}} K_{\free{j}\dum{k}} = K_{\free{j}\dum{k}} Q_{\free{i}\dum{k}} \] \[ S_{\free{bhij}} = Q_{\free{bhi}\dum{k}} K_{\free{bhj}\dum{k}} \]
Contract two axes by giving them the same letter. Batch \(b\) and head \(h\) are just two more free indices.

2Two Habits That Prevent Most Bugs

Habit 1 · Give every axis a permanent letter

b hBatch, query head
gKV head group (GQA / MQA)
iQuery position (output row)
jKey / value position
kQ·K head dim — usually contracted
dValue / output feature dim
m nInner sums (softmax) — never reuse j
X̄Gradient \(\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial X\), same shape as \(X\)

Habit 2 · An index repeats on the RHS? Check the LHS first

\[ P_{\free{i}\free{j}} = \frac{\exp(S_{\free{i}\free{j}})}{\ell_{\free{i}}} \]
\(i\) appears twice on the right — but it's on the left, so it's free and never summed.
  • With an LHS: any index on the LHS is locked as free. In \(D_{\free{i}} = P_{\free{i}\dum{m}}\bar{P}_{\free{i}\dum{m}}\), \(i\) is free and \(m\) is summed.
  • Without an LHS (a standalone expression): parenthesize non-summed repeats, e.g. \(D_{(i)}\bar{P}_{(i)j}\).

3Kronecker Delta: The Engine of Tensor Calculus

Rules 1–2 make forward equations clean. The Kronecker delta \(\kd_{ij}\) is what turns backpropagation from matrix-calculus gymnastics into mechanical renaming.

3.1 · What \(\delta_{ij}\) is: the identity matrix, written as an index

hover a cell
\[ \kd_{ij} = \begin{cases} 1, & i = j \\ 0, & i \neq j \end{cases} \]

Ones on the diagonal, zeros everywhere else — exactly the identity matrix \(I\).

Its superpower appears when it's multiplied into another tensor and summed:

The index renamer
\[ \kd_{\free{i}\dum{j}}\, v_{\dum{j}} = v_{\free{i}} \qquad\qquad \kd_{\dum{m}\free{j}}\, A_{\free{i}\dum{m}} = A_{\free{ij}} \]
Why? Expand the sum: \( \kd_{i\dum{j}} v_{\dum{j}} = \kd_{i1}v_1 + \kd_{i2}v_2 + \dots + \kd_{iL}v_L \) — every term is zero except \(j = i\).

Rule of thumb: δ deletes the sum and swaps the dummy letter for the other one.

3.2 · Where δ comes from: differentiating independent coordinates

Why does \(\delta\) suddenly appear in Softmax and Attention backprop? Because \(S_{i1}, \dots, S_{iL}\) are independent coordinates in row \(i\). Differentiating one by another has only two possible outcomes:

Case 1 · same variable
\( j = m \)
\(S_{im}\) and \(S_{ij}\) are the same variable
\( \dfrac{\partial S_{im}}{\partial S_{ij}} = 1 \)
Case 2 · different variable
\( j \neq m \)
\(S_{im}\) does not depend on \(S_{ij}\)
\( \dfrac{\partial S_{im}}{\partial S_{ij}} = 0 \)
COMBINE BOTH CASES WITH δ
\[ \frac{\partial S_{im}}{\partial S_{ij}} = \kd_{jm} \]

The same logic works at any rank — one δ per pair of matching index slots:

Vector
\( \dfrac{\partial x_i}{\partial x_j} = \kd_{ij} \)
Matrix
\( \dfrac{\partial A_{pq}}{\partial A_{ij}} = \kd_{pi}\kd_{qj} \)
Function
\( \dfrac{\partial f(x_m)}{\partial x_j} = f'(x_m)\,\kd_{mj} \)

4Payoff: Algorithms in One Line

Because every term is a plain scalar, you can freely regroup sums — and sometimes that regrouping is a new algorithm.

Linear Attention: \(O(L^2 D) \to O(L D^2)\) by moving one parenthesis

\[ O_{\free{id}} = \underbrace{\big(Q_{\free{i}\dum{k}} K_{\dum{jk}}\big)}_{L \times L} V_{\dum{j}\free{d}} = Q_{\free{i}\dum{k}} \underbrace{\big(K_{\dum{jk}} V_{\dum{j}\free{d}}\big)}_{D \times D} \]
Sum k first
\(O(L^2 D)\)
Builds an \(L \times L\) attention matrix.
Sum j first
\(O(L D^2)\)
Builds only a tiny \(D \times D\) state \(M_{kd}\).

FlashAttention's core backward identity

Matrix calculus needs trace and Hadamard tricks to show \(\mathrm{rowsum}(P \odot \bar{P}) = \mathrm{rowsum}(O \odot \bar{O})\). In index notation, substitute \(\bar{P}_{im} = \bar{O}_{id} V_{md}\) and regroup:

\[ D_{\free{i}} = P_{\free{i}\dum{m}}\bar{P}_{\free{i}\dum{m}} = P_{\free{i}\dum{m}}\big(\bar{O}_{\free{i}\dum{d}} V_{\dum{md}}\big) = \big(P_{\free{i}\dum{m}} V_{\dum{md}}\big)\bar{O}_{\free{i}\dum{d}} = O_{\free{i}\dum{d}}\,\bar{O}_{\free{i}\dum{d}} \]

One line of grade-school associativity — and \(D_i\) can now be computed from \(O\) and \(\bar{O}\) without ever materializing \(P\).
